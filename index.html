An industry consolidating from the top

Nine of ten US specialty contractors employ fewer than 20 people, yet the share of employment held by firms with 500 or more employees has doubled since 1998. The panels below track that reallocation, the thinning pipeline of new firms, and what has and has not happened to pay. Every series is computed from public Census Bureau and BLS data.

Pick a trade and a state, enter an hourly wage, and see where it falls in the official BLS wage distribution for that occupation. Useful for hiring offers, raise conversations, and relocation decisions. Data: OEWS state percentiles, 2003-2024.

Percentile placement is interpolated between the published 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles; wages outside that range are reported as below the 10th or above the 90th. OEWS covers all employers of the occupation, union and non-union, across industries.

How long do contracting firms last?

Firm dissolution in building equipment contracting is almost entirely a small-firm event: about 7 in every 100 firms with fewer than 20 employees close for good each year, against fewer than 1 in 100 mid-size firms and essentially zero large ones. This tool turns those observed rates into survival odds over a planning horizon. It is an actuarial baseline, not a prediction for any particular firm.

Survival compounds the mean annual firm death rate observed over the last five BDS years for the chosen size class. A firm death requires every establishment of the firm to close; a firm that is acquired and keeps operating is not a death. Source: BDS firm-size files, NAICS 2382, 1978-2023.

Where do markets like yours go next?

Every US county is classed by how many electrical (or plumbing/HVAC) establishments it hosts. Tracking every county across three long windows gives the empirical odds of a market growing, shrinking, or holding its class: the same Markov matrices as in the research paper, harmonised across the 2017 disclosure redesign.

Counties below three establishments are unpublished after the 2017 CBP redesign, so the under-3 class cannot be tracked into 2017–2023 and its row mixes true zeros with censoring in earlier windows. Connecticut’s 2022 switch from counties to planning regions produces a handful of artificial exits in the last window.

About

Everything on this site is computed from public federal statistical data and reproduces series reported in the companion research manuscript on the industrial organization of the US specialty trades. Series that cross the 2017 County Business Patterns disclosure redesign are harmonised by applying the post-2017 three-establishment publication floor to every year.
